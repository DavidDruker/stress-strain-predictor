Yield strength — Tensile strength — Cross-section 78.5 mm²
  • Only three points are predicted — yield, maximum load and fracture. The curve drawn between them is a Hollomon power law pinned to those points, not a prediction: no open dataset reports strain at maximum load.
  • Chemistry cannot see heat treatment. The same steel tempered at 200 °C and at 650 °C differs by 500 MPa or more and reaches the model as the same input. Read this as what steels of roughly this chemistry usually do.
  • It is already at its ceiling. Bars of identical chemistry scatter 107–191 MPa in yield strength, because they differ by processing. The model sits inside that band, so the error is the data's, not the model's.
Further limits
  • On four of twelve held-out alloy families it did worse than guessing the training median. The check beside the inputs catches out-of-range elements, but a chemistry can sit inside every range and still belong to an unfamiliar family.
  • Elongation is only comparable within a gauge-length standard, and the training data does not record which one each row used. Millimetres of stretch inherit that; the forces do not.
  • Trained on 1,359 heats, 55% of them specification minima rather than measurements. Not a substitute for a tensile test, and not for design allowables.
